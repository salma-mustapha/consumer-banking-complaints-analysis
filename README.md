Consumer Banking Complaint Analysis

Consumer complaints provide insight into recurring problems consumers experience with financial products and services, making complaint patterns and resolution outcomes important indicators of consumer and operational concerns. This project analyzes 55,271 consumer complaints submitted to the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau (CFPB) from May through July 2026 to evaluate complaint volume, banking products, consumer issues, company response outcomes, response timeliness, and monetary relief.

Using SQL, Python, and Power BI, the analysis examines which products and issues generate the greatest complaint volume, which companies receive the most complaints, and how resolution outcomes differ across products. SQL was used for data validation and analysis, while Power BI was used to communicate key findings. Python was used to statistically test the relationship between product type and resolution outcome and to analyze consumer complaint narratives using TF-IDF. The analysis found a statistically significant but relatively weak association between product type and resolution outcome (p < 0.001; Cramér's V = 0.114), with credit-card complaints more strongly associated with monetary relief.

The analysis found that credit card and checking or savings account complaints accounted for approximately 76% of all complaints, while companies provided timely responses to 99.07% of complaints. However, monetary relief varied substantially by product, ranging from 12.63% for credit cards to 1.26% for mortgages. Narrative analysis provided additional context, with monetary-relief complaints relatively more associated with fraud, charges, and refunds, while explanation-based complaints were more associated with merchant investigations, reviews, disputed transactions, and responses. The project concludes by recommending greater attention to recurring complaint drivers, credit-card dispute resolution, and evaluating resolution outcomes alongside response timeliness.
